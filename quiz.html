Notes on the decks

Each explanation ends with the page of that lecture's PDF it comes from. Questions marked From the JVP handout test the JVP handout rather than the lecture decks.

A few points in the decks differ from current practice. No question depends on them:

  • 2:1 AV block (L11 p. 13). Listed under Mobitz II. A 2:1 pattern alone cannot tell Mobitz I from Mobitz II.
  • Ventricular tachycardia (L12 p. 9). Defined as a rate above 120 with no P waves. Current guidelines use a rate above 100, and P waves can still be present.
  • Cardiac arrest (L12 p. 13). Described as a flat line. A flat line is asystole; cardiac arrest also includes VF, pulseless VT and PEA.
  • Tricuspid atresia (L15 p. 7). Said to need both an ASD and a VSD. An atrial opening is essential; lung blood flow can come through a VSD and/or a PDA.
  • QT interval (L10 p. 7). Given as a fixed 10–11 small boxes. QT shortens as heart rate rises, so clinicians use a rate-corrected QT (QTc).
  • Cycle length (L13 p. 4). 60/72 is 0.83 s, not 0.8 s; 0.8 s corresponds to 75 bpm.
  • Pause after a PAC (L12 p. 4). Called compensatory. A PAC usually resets the SA node, so the textbook pause is non-compensatory.
